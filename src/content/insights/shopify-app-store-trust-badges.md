The Built for Shopify badge tells you an app has cleared Shopify's higher bar for quality, performance, and integration, but it is a quality signal, not a security clearance, and it does not promise the app will run fast on your specific store or handle your data the way you would want.

## Key takeaways

- Built for Shopify (BFS) is an earned, revocable certification that an app meets Shopify's elevated standards for performance, embedding, API use, and merchant experience.
- It is a genuine positive signal. All else equal, a BFS app is a safer starting point than an uncertified one.
- It does not audit how the app handles your customer data, and it is not a substitute for checking the app's permissions.
- It does not guarantee the app will be fast on your store, because your theme plus that app is a combination the badge never tested.
- Treat the badge as one input, then do your own two checks: [what the app can access](/insights/audit-shopify-app-permissions) and what it costs your store speed.

## What the Built for Shopify badge is

Built for Shopify is a certification Shopify awards to apps that meet a raised set of standards, above the baseline required just to be listed in the App Store. It shows up as a badge on the app's listing and is meant to help merchants spot apps that Shopify considers well built.

Earning it is not automatic and it is not permanent. An app has to demonstrate that it meets criteria across several areas, and Shopify can remove the badge if the app slips below the bar later. That makes it more meaningful than a listing alone, because a listed app has only cleared the minimum, while a BFS app has cleared a higher, ongoing standard.

The important framing is that BFS is Shopify grading the app against Shopify's own definition of a good app. That definition is heavily about integration quality and merchant experience. It is a useful lens, but it is Shopify's lens, focused on how well the app fits the platform, not a full independent audit of everything you might care about as the merchant carrying the risk.

## What the badge actually certifies

The badge speaks to a cluster of quality signals that genuinely matter.

It certifies performance discipline. BFS apps are expected to keep their impact on your storefront and admin low, using efficient loading and avoiding the kind of heavy scripts that drag down page speed. This is real and worth having.

It certifies proper embedding. A BFS app runs inside the Shopify admin the way modern apps should, using current authentication and the embedded app framework rather than clunky redirects or outdated patterns. That means a smoother, more secure admin experience.

It certifies current API use and stability. The app is expected to use up-to-date APIs, handle them well, and not break as Shopify evolves. It also reflects a baseline of merchant experience: a clean install, sensible onboarding, responsive support, and a decent review history.

Taken together, that is a meaningful bar. An app that holds a BFS badge has shown it is built with care and keeps its platform footprint reasonable. When you are choosing between two apps that do the same job, the badge is a fair tiebreaker.

## What the badge does not certify

Here is where merchants overtrust it. The badge covers integration quality, but several things you care about sit outside its scope.

It does not audit your data handling. The badge is not a security review of how the app collects, stores, shares, or protects your customer data once it has access. An app can be beautifully built, perfectly embedded, and still request far more data access than its job needs. The badge does not stop that, which is exactly why [app permission audits](/insights/audit-shopify-app-permissions) stay your responsibility.

It does not guarantee speed on your store. BFS performance criteria are assessed in general terms. Your store is a specific combination of theme, other apps, and configuration that the badge never tested. An app that is light on a clean demo store can still be the thing that pushes your particular storefront past its performance budget, especially stacked with everything else you run.

It does not vet the vendor's wider practices or fit. The badge does not tell you whether the vendor shares data with third parties beyond what Shopify requires, whether the pricing suits you, or whether this is even the right app for your use case. And a great app you do not need is still an app slowing your store and widening your risk surface.

## How to use the badge in a smart install decision

Use it as a filter, not a final answer. The badge narrows the field to better-built candidates; your own checks pick the winner and keep it honest.

Prefer BFS apps when the choice is otherwise close. If two apps solve your problem and one is Built for Shopify, that is a reasonable reason to lean toward it, because it has cleared a higher, monitored bar.

Then run the two checks the badge cannot do for you. First, review what the app requests access to before you approve it, and reject anything that wants more than its function needs. Second, measure your store speed before and after installing. When you [scan with Store Auditor](/how-it-works), you can see exactly how much a newly installed app, badge or no badge, adds to your load, so a certified app that quietly slows your checkout does not get a free pass.

Finally, keep watching after install. Badges can be revoked, apps change, and an app that was light at install can get heavier with updates. A store that reviews its installed apps periodically catches the drift. For merchants who would rather have this handled, the team at [Defyn](https://defyn.com.au) includes an app review, badge or not, in every store audit, weighing each app on real permissions and real performance rather than the badge alone.

## Common questions

### Is a Built for Shopify app safe to install?

It is a safer starting point than an uncertified app, but safe is not the right word for any app on its own. The Built for Shopify badge certifies quality, embedding, and performance discipline, so a BFS app is likely well built and reasonably light. What the badge does not do is audit how the app handles your data or guarantee it will be fast on your specific store. Treat the badge as a positive signal, then still review the app's data permissions and measure its impact on your store speed before you rely on it.

### What does the Built for Shopify badge require?

An app earns the badge by clearing a higher bar than a normal App Store listing across several areas: keeping its performance impact on your storefront and admin low, embedding properly in the admin with current authentication, using up-to-date APIs, and delivering a solid merchant experience including clean install, onboarding, support, and reviews. It is assessed on an ongoing basis, and Shopify can revoke the badge if the app falls below the standard. That is why it carries more weight than a listing alone, which only reflects the minimum requirements.

### Should I only install Built for Shopify apps?

Not necessarily. The badge is a useful tiebreaker, but plenty of excellent apps are not certified, sometimes simply because the vendor has not gone through the process, and some certified apps may not fit your needs or your store's performance budget. Use the badge to lean toward better-built apps when the choice is close, but base the final decision on what the app actually accesses, how it performs on your store, and whether you genuinely need it. The badge informs the decision; it should not make it for you.

## Wrap-up

The Built for Shopify badge is worth paying attention to. It marks apps that have cleared a real, monitored standard for performance, embedding, and merchant experience, and between two similar apps it is a fair reason to prefer the certified one. What it is not is a security clearance or a promise about your specific store. It does not audit the app's access to your data, and it does not guarantee the app stays light once it is running alongside everything else you have installed. So use the badge as a filter, then do the two things it cannot: check [what each app can actually access](/insights/identify-malicious-shopify-apps) and measure what it costs your store speed. The badge gets you to a shorter list of good candidates. Your own checks keep the one you install honest.
